The book is a flat array, not a tree

Each side of each symbol's book is a dense band of price levels indexed by tick offset — O(1) lookup, and the levels near the touch share cache lines. Orders are 64-byte pooled nodes on intrusive FIFOs. A robin-hood hash map with backward-shift deletion finds any order by reference in ~12ns; over a full day it never rehashed and never probed past 10 slots.

The ring's ordering is argued, not assumed

The SPSC ring uses release/acquire publication with relaxed self-reads, cached indices to kill cross-core line ping-pong, and batched pops. The memory-ordering proof is written out in the header; a TSan-instrumented stress test (5M assertions) is treated as supporting evidence — not the proof itself.

Correctness without an answer key

A deliberately-simple std::map reference book is kept forever as the oracle. The fast book must match it on every level and every queue position over the entire day — plus golden snapshots, structural invariants, property tests on the matcher, fuzz targets, and an audit derived from the stream itself.

03

What real data taught me

Finding · memory

The design doc's band sizing was wrong by 16× — and the measurement caught it

Price bands anchor on each side's first order. Pre-open, that's often a $0.01 bid on a $300 stock — stranding the band miles from real trading. The doc's default configuration allocated 31 GB of price-level arrays; retuned, the same throughput fits in 1.9 GB. The proper fix — re-centring bands on actual activity — is now queued with data to justify it, which is exactly the order I wanted decisions made in.

Finding · microstructure

The audit's "failures" turned out to be market microstructure

If price-time priority holds, an executed order should be at the front of its queue. Overall pass rate: 98.53%. Split by execution type it becomes an explanation: at-price executions pass 99.87%, while price-improved executions pass only 17% — correctly, because midpoint prints don't follow displayed-queue priority. The audit didn't find a bug; it found the market.

05

Design decisions, briefly defended

Why no PriceLevel backpointer in Order?
With a flat band, (side, price) is the pointer — O(1) arithmetic to the level. Kills the rebase fix-up problem outright; the overflow map pays O(log n) on its rare path instead.
Why backward-shift deletion, not tombstones?
Deletes are as frequent as inserts over a replay day. Tombstones degrade probe lengths monotonically; backward shift keeps clusters canonical — max probe 10 after 423M messages.
Why is Replace composed in the engine?
ITCH's Replace carries no side — it must be inherited from the original. Writing snapshot→remove→add once, above both book implementations, means the rule can't drift between them.
Why does the matcher reuse book primitives?
Match mode is composed from the same verified operations reconstruct uses, and it's generic over the book — so the oracle differential covers matching too, fill-for-fill.
Why release/acquire and not seq_cst?
Release publishes the slot write; acquire observes it. That's the entire requirement. On x86 both compile to plain MOVs — seq_cst would buy an mfence and nothing else.
Why keep the slow reference book forever?
It's the oracle. Every optimisation must reproduce it exactly — level, queue, counter — over a full real day, every night, in CI.
